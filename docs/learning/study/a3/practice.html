Objectives

  • Put a response in one of the six categories by the grader's ordered rules, and say how the category changes with what was rendered.
  • Compute design effect, effective size and a 95% half-width for given S, N and ρ, and say what one more repetition buys.
  • Say in two sentences each why a failed H2 equivalence part is not an effect and why H5 is an intersection-union test.
  • Work Holm's thresholds over five and over four tests, and the smallest p-value a bootstrap of B draws can give.
  • Work main effects and a count × richness interaction by hand from eight cell rates, and say why the scale is fixed before the data.
  • Design a test that separates "the model reads the schema" from "the decoder enforces it", and say what it cannot show.

Check yourself first

Three questions the study page prepared you for. Answer out loud, then open.

1A cell has 400 calls from 20 scenarios and about 20% of them fail. Is its 95% interval about ±4 points, or far wider, and what decides which?

Show the answer

It depends on how failures cluster within scenarios (the correlation ρ between reruns). About ±4 points only if reruns are independent. The page's constructed example: the same 80 failures give 16% to 24% when spread and 5% to 40% when four scenarios fail every rerun.

If you missed it, review The unit of independent variation is the scenario.

2A service returns a 400 with a field error, and another call times out. Which do you retry unchanged, and what must be true first?

Show the answer

The timeout, if the call is idempotent; the field error will be rejected the same way every time. This unit's recovery measure counts only the persistent error kinds as an identical-retry loop.

If you missed it, review Recovery, and the identical retry.

3A contract test checks a consumer's request. Against which schema: the subset the docs showed, or the provider's real one?

Show the answer

The provider's real contract. This study grades every call against one canonical strict schema, however much of it the model was shown, so a loose schema is charged for constraints it never showed.

If you missed it, review Strictness is schema content, and every call is graded against one canonical schema.

Estimation drills

Do the arithmetic on paper first, then open the worked steps.

What is a 20 × 20 cell worth at three correlations?

A cell has 20 scenarios × 20 repetitions = 400 calls and a pooled failure rate of 20%. Give the design effect, the effective size and the 95% half-width (normal approximation) at ρ = 0, 0.6 and 0.9. Then say how many repetitions the sizing rule picks at ρ = 0.6, with a floor of 5 and a cap of 20.

Worked answer
  1. Independent standard error: √(0.2 × 0.8 / 400) = 0.0200, so the independent half-width is 1.96 × 0.0200 = ±3.9 points.
  2. Design effect 1 + (N − 1)ρ = 1 + 19ρ: 1 at ρ = 0, 12.4 at 0.6, 18.1 at 0.9.
  3. Effective size 400 / design effect: 400, 32.3 and 22.1.
  4. Half-width = 3.9 × √design effect: ±3.9, ±13.8 and ±16.7 points. The same 400 calls give an interval more than four times wider at ρ = 0.9.
  5. Sizing rule: the smallest N reaching 95% of the limit S/ρ, so N ≥ 19(1 − ρ)/ρ = 19 × 0.4 / 0.6 = 12.67, rounded up to 13 (inside 5 to 20).

Answer. Design effect 1, 12.4, 18.1; effective size 400, 32.3, 22.1; half-width ±3.9, ±13.8, ±16.7 points; N = 13 at ρ = 0.6. Say it as: 400 calls are somewhere between 20 and 400 facts.

Contrasts and an interaction from eight illustrative rates

ILLUSTRATIVE rates, not data or predictions. Failure rate (%) by cell. Low count: terse-loose 30, terse-strict 20, rich-loose 26, rich-strict 16. High count: terse-loose 50, terse-strict 40, rich-loose 34, rich-strict 24. Find the three main effects and the count × richness interaction as the repo defines it, and check it with the code's weights.

Worked answer
  1. Main effect of count = mean(high) − mean(low) = 148 / 4 − 92 / 4 = 37 − 23 = +14 points.
  2. Richness: mean(rich) − mean(terse) = 100 / 4 − 140 / 4 = 25 − 35 = −10 points. Strictness: strict 25, loose 35, so also −10.
  3. Richness effect at low count = 21 − 25 = −4 points; at high count = 29 − 45 = −16 points.
  4. Interaction = effect at high − effect at low = −16 − (−4) = −12 points (the difference, not halved).
  5. Check with weights s_count × s_richness / 2 over the eight cells: [(30 + 20 + 34 + 24) − (26 + 16 + 50 + 40)] / 2 = (108 − 132) / 2 = −12.
  6. Strictness × count: effect −10 at high count and −10 at low count, so the interaction is 0.
  7. Scale: suppose terse 20% falls to rich 10% at low count and 40% to 20% at high count. Risk difference: −20 − (−10) = −10 points of interaction. Ratio: rich halves failures at both counts, so no interaction.

Answer. Main effects +14, −10, −10 points; count × richness −12 points (full difference, not halved); strictness × count 0. An interaction on the point scale can vanish on the ratio scale, so the scale is fixed before the data.

Deep-dive drill

Ordered easy to hard. Answer out loud, open the model answer, then the red-flag answer: the tempting reply that sounds right and is not.

Drill 1 of 12

The model is shown two tools and calls a third that exists in your library but was not shown. Category? And if the cell shows 20 tools including it?

Model answer

MALFORMED: a tool that was not rendered is a structural failure, and structure is checked first. In the 20-tool cell the same call is WRONG_TOOL; the grader records which kind (confusable, filler or low-set tool). The category depends on what was rendered, by design.

Red-flag answer

"WRONG_TOOL, it is a real tool." The grader asks what the model was shown, not what exists.

Drill 2 of 12

The user only says thanks, so no call is expected, and the model calls a shown tool with a required argument missing. Category?

Model answer

MALFORMED. The structure check runs before the no-call check, so a broken call is MALFORMED whatever the intent. A well-formed call in the same scenario would be SPURIOUS. Order is part of the contract.

Red-flag answer

"SPURIOUS, it should not have called." That reads intent; the grader applies ordered rules.

Drill 3 of 12

Why is a loose-schema call graded against the strict schema? Isn't that unfair to the loose arm?

Model answer

It is the measurement: what a loose schema costs is exactly the constraints the model was not told. Grading against the rendered schema would give the strict arm more ways to fail, so strictness would look like it causes the failures it constrains, a confound the instrument would create. Rich text can state a constraint in words, which is part of the richness and strictness overlap.

Red-flag answer

"We grade against what the model saw, to be fair."

Drill 4 of 12

Provider strict mode is on and MALFORMED is zero. What have you learned about the model's structural ability?

Model answer

Very little: constrained decoding makes a conforming call nearly certain by construction, so a zero measures the decoder. The vendors' exceptions as the amendment read them (refusals, truncation, enum capitalisation) were not re-checked here. What stays informative is WRONG_TOOL, value-level WRONG_PARAMS, MISSING and SPURIOUS, which is why strict mode is off in the eight cells and run as one labelled exploratory slice.

Red-flag answer

"The model is perfectly reliable at formatting."

Drill 5 of 12

A cell has 20 scenarios × 20 repetitions. Why is its interval not based on 400 observations, and what do you resample?

Model answer

Reruns of a scenario are correlated, so the effective size is S·N / (1 + (N − 1)ρ), between S and S·N: at ρ = 0.6 about 32.3, not 400, and the half-width is about ±13.8 points against ±3.9 for independent calls (normal approximation, 20% failure rate). Resample whole scenarios, recomputing every cell rate from the drawn set; Wilson is for one scenario's repetitions only.

Red-flag answer

"Wilson on 80 / 400." It sees only 80 and 400 and gives the same narrow answer for the spread and the concentrated dataset.

Drill 6 of 12

At ρ = 0.9 and S = 30, what does going from 5 to 40 repetitions buy? What does this design's pilot imply for N?

Model answer

Almost nothing: effective size rises from 32.6 to 33.2 (amendment §4d), under 2%. The information is in the scenarios, so spend on S. Separately, the band keeps baseline rates between 0.10 and 0.90 over 10 reruns, which caps the realised ρ near 0.61, so the rule for N gives at least 13 (12.15 rounded up).

Red-flag answer

"More repetitions always tighten the interval."

Drill 7 of 12

Holm at 0.05 over five hypotheses with add-one bootstrap p-values. What is the smallest p-value you can get, and why does the pre-registration refuse fewer than 100 draws?

Model answer

With B draws the smallest p is 1/(B + 1). Holm's first threshold is 0.05 / 5 = 0.01, so B must be at least 99 for any hypothesis to be rejectable at all; at B = 10,000 the floor is about 0.0001. The thresholds are 0.01, 0.0125, 0.0167, 0.025 and 0.05; over four tests (H4 not testable) they are 0.0125, 0.0167, 0.025 and 0.05. The code's MIN_ITERATIONS is 100.

Red-flag answer

"Any number of draws is fine, the correction is just a threshold."

Drill 8 of 12

H2's equivalence part fails. Has strictness been shown to move WRONG_TOOL?

Model answer

No. Failing to show equivalence is inconclusive, not an effect: the code reports failed_part = "equivalence" when Holm rejects the difference part but the 90% interval is not inside ±δ₂ (proposed 2 points). As a rough bound (normal approximation, true effect zero) it passes only if the across-scenario SD of the strictness contrast is under about 6.0 points at S = 24, 6.7 at S = 30.

Red-flag answer

"Strictness affects tool choice." Wide intervals are not an effect.

Drill 9 of 12

Why is H5 an intersection-union test, and what went wrong with the first version?

Model answer

H5 compares |count effect| with |A/A contrast|. With near-deterministic reruns the A/A contrast is about zero, so the statistic reduces to |count effect| and the one-sided p-value collapses to about 1/(B + 1) whatever the data say: a simulated null count effect was "supported" 80% of the time (amendment §14 item 8). So H5 also needs a two-sided test that the count effect is not zero, and its p-value is the larger of the two.

Red-flag answer

"The A/A contrast is a fine baseline on its own."

Drill 10 of 12

The amendment expects near-deterministic reruns, yet the band keeps scenarios with a 10% to 90% baseline pass rate. Reconcile them.

Model answer

The sources do not reconcile them; this is a reading, not a finding. A rate strictly between 0 and 1 over 10 reruns means the reruns disagreed, so the band also selects for flipping. If reruns barely flip, few candidates may survive and authoring more may not help; the pilot's survival count measures which regime holds, so it is pending. A flip fraction measured on a band-selected set may also not be comparable with the Noise Floor Audit's; that is a reading, not something the sources state.

Red-flag answer

"The band just picks medium-difficulty scenarios." It picks scenarios whose reruns disagree.

Drill 11 of 12

A teammate prices retries as c / (1 − f). In an illustrative case (f = 0.3, up to three attempts) the formula gives 1.43 calls per success. What is wrong with it?

Model answer

It assumes each retry is an independent draw. If the 30% of scenarios that fail always fail, a group spends 0.7 × 1 + 0.3 × 3 = 1.6 calls, only 70% of groups succeed, and cost per success is 1.6 / 0.7 = 2.29: the formula understates it by 37.5%. The study walks each scenario's recorded reruns in order, which is the scenario-as-unit argument in money.

Red-flag answer

"Retries are geometric, so 1 / (1 − f)."

Drill 12 of 12

Design a test that separates "the model reads the schema" from "the decoder enforces it", and one that separates "rich descriptions disambiguate" from "rich descriptions restate the enum".

Model answer

First: a 2 × 2 of schema content (loose, strict) × constrained decoding (off, on) on the same scenarios. The repo runs strict content with decoding off (the eight cells) and one exploratory slice with decoding on; the other corners are not run, and loose content with decoding on may not be expressible where strict mode needs every property required (the amendment's reading of OpenAI, not re-checked). Second: a richness level whose rich text omits enum values and patterns; the deviations log notes units' rich text lists the enum, so there strictness adds nothing. Label it exploratory.

Red-flag answer

"Just compare strict against loose." That mixes content and decoding.

Design scenario

A fictional travel marketplace exposes 60 tools to one booking agent: flight search, fare rules, seat maps, hotel search, itinerary changes, loyalty points and more. Wrong-tool calls and badly formatted dates are the top support complaints. Leadership asks: "Should we turn on provider strict mode and cut the agent to 20 tools?" Design how you would decide and how you would roll it out. Bring nothing from any employer.

Outline of a strong answer
  1. Turn the question into failure categories first: grade a sample of traces with a deterministic taxonomy. If most failures are WRONG_TOOL, constrained decoding will not fix them; disambiguation and fewer or better-separated tools might.
  2. Offline factorial on held-out scenarios: tool count (60 against a routed 20) × schema content × constrained decoding on or off, with deliberate look-alikes (fare rules against fare search). Only calls whose correct form can be listed in advance.
  3. The unit is the scenario: size repetitions from a pilot's ρ, average per scenario, bootstrap over scenarios, report per-category rates with intervals and the exclusion rate.
  4. Cost per successful call with the real retry policy, walked from recorded reruns, and prompt caching measured rather than assumed. A wrong action (a cancelled booking) is not a retry.
  5. Guardrails beyond the prompt: validate against the canonical schema at the boundary; idempotency keys on side-effecting tools so a retry after a timeout cannot act twice; actionable error text (the page predicts, but does not show, that it reduces identical retries).
  6. Rollout: shadow-grade the new presentation on live traffic with no side effects, then canary with per-category alerts, pin model versions and re-run the suite on every change.
Pitfalls to check your answer against
  • Treating repetitions as independent and reporting a Wilson interval over all calls.
  • Grading against the schema the model was shown, so the loose arm is forgiven.
  • Turning strict mode on and calling a zero malformed rate a model improvement.
  • Claiming results beyond structurally checkable single calls, or quoting a prediction as a finding.

Lab

No cost

Goal. See offline that the category depends on the rendering, that the interval depends on how failures split across scenarios, and that correlated failures break the retry formula.

Zero spend: no API key is in the environment and no step makes a model call. The dry run's shares are planted by a stub and say nothing about any model; do not copy them anywhere.

Setup

  • Work in a scratch directory outside the repo. Run every script as env -u OPENAI_API_KEY -u ANTHROPIC_API_KEY PYTHONPATH=<repo> <repo>/.venv/bin/python -B lab.py, so no key reaches the process.
  • Never use uv run (it re-syncs the environment) and install nothing. Read toolcall/README.md, "How values are compared", first.

Steps

  1. Grade by hand, then by machine. Build the 12 responses of the boundary-cases table with toolcall.responses.openai_call, openai_text and toolcall.providers.openai_parse (tests/test_toolcall_grader.py has the calls). Write your predicted category first, then call toolcall.grader.grade.
  2. Same call, two renderings. Load toolcall/data/tools and toolcall/data/scenarios (load_library, load_scenarios). For weather-forecast-lisbon grade get_current_weather with location Lisbon using toolcall.formulation.tool_names(scenario, library, "low"), then "high".
  3. Clustering. Build two datasets of 20 scenarios × 20 reruns with 80 failures each: spread (0 to 7 per scenario) and concentrated (4 scenarios fail 20 of 20, 16 fail 0). Call toolcall.effects.wilson(80, 400), cluster_bootstrap(rates, average, iterations=10000, seed=1) and toolcall.budget.icc.
  4. Sweep ρ. For ρ in 0.3, 0.5, 0.61, 0.7 and 0.9 print toolcall.budget.reps_for(rho, 5, 20) and effective_n(30, n, rho) for n = 5, 13 and 20.
  5. Retry walk. In plain Python make 30 scenarios × 21 reruns at an assumed failure rate of 0.3, once independent and once with 9 scenarios that always fail. Cut each scenario into groups of three, spend until the first success, and divide spend by wins.
  6. Dry run on the shipped suite (stub, NOT DATA): scripts/a3_diagnose.py --tools toolcall/data/tools --scenarios toolcall/data/scenarios --dry-run --reps 3 --store <scratch>/dry.jsonl. Only the fact that it loads, renders and grades matters.

Record

  • Predicted against graded category, for the 12 responses.
  • The category of one call in the low and the high rendering.
  • Wilson interval, bootstrap interval and ICC for each dataset.
  • N the rule picks, and effective size, for each ρ.
  • Calls per success and share of groups that succeed, independent and always-fail.
Expected result and what it shows

Expected. 1: the table. 2: MALFORMED (not in context) in the low cell, WRONG_TOOL (confusable) in the high cell. 3: Wilson 0.164 to 0.242 for both; bootstrap about 0.16 to 0.24 spread, 0.05 to 0.40 concentrated; ICC about 0.004 against 1.0. 4: N = 20, 19, 13, 9, 5; effective size at ρ = 0.9 is 32.6 at N = 5, 33.1 at N = 20. 5: about 1.43 calls per success, 97% of groups succeeding, when independent (seed-dependent); 2.29 and 70% when 9 scenarios always fail. 6: 147 graded calls.

Shows. The category is a function of the rendering plus one canonical schema; the interval is a function of how failures cluster; retry economics follow the clustering. None of it needs a model call.

Recall cards

taxonomyOrder the six categories by grader precedence.
MALFORMED, SPURIOUS, MISSING, WRONG_TOOL, WRONG_PARAMS, CORRECT. The first rule that applies wins.
taxonomyWhich three outcomes are excluded before any rate, and how are they reported?
API_ERROR, REFUSED and TRUNCATED: counted per cell and never graded; a cell where they together exceed 2% of its calls is flagged.
taxonomyNo call at all where a call was expected. Category?
MISSING, never MALFORMED. A call written as text is graded the same way (flagged, not a call).
taxonomyWhat does each failure category point at?
MALFORMED: schema strictness, output format. SPURIOUS: description scoping, when not to call. MISSING: discoverability, richer descriptions. WRONG_TOOL: disambiguation, fewer tools. WRONG_PARAMS: typing strictness, parameter naming (toolcall README).
taxonomyName the three kinds of WRONG_TOOL the grader records.
confusable (a look-alike written for the scenario), filler (another tool in the 20), and low_set (one of the scenario's own tools that is not acceptable). The amendment names two.
canonical"3" is sent for an integer parameter. Category and rule?
MALFORMED, rule R4: a number sent as a string is a type error, so structural. 3.0 for an integer is accepted under R3 when it has no fractional part.
canonicalAn enum value outside the set, or a pattern miss. Category?
WRONG_PARAMS: value-level, not structural. A key the schema does not define is also WRONG_PARAMS (R7).
canonicalA null for an optional parameter, and a null for a required one?
R5: null for an optional parameter equals omitting it; null for a required one counts as the parameter missing, so MALFORMED.
cellsWhat does the high-count cell contain?
Exactly 20 tools: the scenario's own 2 to 4, its 4 to 6 look-alikes (4 or 5 in the current suite), and filler from its own domain first, then others.
cellsWhat does a loose schema keep in required?
Only the tool's first required parameter; strict renders enums, patterns, every required parameter and additionalProperties: false.
cellsWhy is provider strict mode off in all eight cells?
It would make MALFORMED nearly impossible by construction and test the decoder, not the model. One labelled exploratory slice turns it on.
clusterEffective sample size, and what it approaches.
S·N / (1 + (N − 1)ρ), approaching S/ρ as N grows. Another repetition buys almost nothing at high ρ, so the information is in the scenarios.
clusterThe N rule.
The smallest N reaching 95% of S/ρ: N ≥ 19(1 − ρ)/ρ, between a floor of 5 and a cap of 20 (toolcall/budget.py).
clusterWilson interval for 0 failures in 20 reruns of one scenario?

contrastsA main effect, in words. Is the interaction halved here?
High-level rate minus low-level rate, averaged over the other two factors, in points. The interaction is the effect at the other factor's high level minus its effect at the low level: not halved. Say which convention you mean.
hypothesesH4's floor rule.
If the opaque arm's identical-retry rate is under 5%, report H4 as not testable at this rate. It counts neither as support nor refutation, and Holm runs over the other four.

After a rejection an identical retry is a loop; after a timeout it is the right move if the call is idempotent. Counting both would blur the pathology.
